17Common pitfalls & debugging

The fountain keeps only its newest sparks, or turns into several fountains taking turns

Symptom: a few hundred particles near the emitter and nothing older; or a pool that seems to advance in fits, with particles from several "generations" interleaved.
Cause: the pool's read-write binding has cycle = true. The step reads what the last step wrote, and a cycled buffer's contents are undefined — SDL hands the pass another internal buffer, empty or holding an older history (§10.2).
Fix: cycle = false for any buffer a pass reads and writes. Declare it with keep_buffer and the frame graph derives it.

Particles fly off in wrong directions at wrong speeds, differently for each one

Symptom: every particle is plausible and every one is wrong; the error grows along the buffer.
Cause: a storage struct with a float3 followed by another float3. C++ puts the second at byte 12, the SPIR-V path at 16, so the stride differs and every record after the first is read from the wrong offset (§4.2).
Fix: rows of float3 + one scalar, or float4s; name any padding explicitly on both sides; static_assert every offset in C++ and read one record back from the GPU once.

The kernel runs and its uniforms are all zero

Symptom: particles spawn at the origin with no velocity, or nothing ever spawns.
Cause: the cbuffer is declared at b0, space1 — a vertex shader's uniform space — or space3. A compute shader's uniforms are b[n], space2 (§4.4).
Fix: register(b0, space2), and check the reflection: "uniform_buffers": 1 is necessary but not sufficient, because a wrong space still reflects a uniform block.

The first frame is full of sparks in random places

Symptom: a burst of particles everywhere on the first frame, never repeated.
Cause: the pool was not zeroed. SDL promises nothing about a new buffer's contents, and random ages and lives make some records alive.
Fix: upload zeros once at creation, as gpu_particles::create does. A zeroed record is dead by construction (§4.1).

Nothing is drawn, or everything is drawn twice, on Windows or Linux only

Symptom: correct on a Mac; no particles, or a flickering count, on Vulkan or Direct3D 12.
Cause: the clear and the compaction are in one compute pass. Metal's serial encoder orders the two dispatches; Vulkan and D3D12 do not, and the atomics race the clear (§10.1).
Fix: one pass per dependent dispatch. Declared through the frame graph, a pass that reads a version another pass produced is ordered after it and in its own pass by construction.

The count is a few hundred when it should be tens of thousands

Symptom: a sparse fountain; the draw's instance count varies wildly frame to frame.
Cause: the compaction increments with args[1] = args[1] + 1. Lanes read the same value together, and most increments are lost (§9.2: 296 to 509 of 1,048,576).
Fix: InterlockedAdd(args[1], 1u, place), and use place as the index into the list.

The draw crashes the device or draws nothing, and the buffer looks right

Symptom: validation errors naming the indirect buffer, or a device loss.
Cause: the arguments buffer lacks SDL_GPU_BUFFERUSAGE_INDIRECT, or lacks COMPUTE_STORAGE_READ beside _WRITE for the atomic's read; or first_vertex/first_instance are not zero while the vertex shader uses the built-in IDs.
Fix: all three usage bits (§9.4), and a clear kernel that writes all four words.

The fountain comes out in horizontal layers

Symptom: shells a step's travel apart, most visible with a narrow speed range.
Cause: every birth of a step is spawned at age zero at the emitter.
Fix: birth_age: each birth advanced by its own age within the step (§6.4).

Sparks vanish in mid-air at a fixed height

Symptom: the fountain is truncated; its top looks cut off.
Cause: the ring overwrites living particles — the pool is smaller than rate × longest life.
Fix: capacity / rate > life_max. At 32,768 slots and 20,000 a second, 76.4% of particles died early (§6.3).

A streak across the screen at every birth

Symptom: thin lines from random points to the emitter, one frame long.
Cause: a respawned slot keeps the old tenant's previous, and the draw interpolates between two different particles.
Fix: set previous to the emitter at spawn (§7.5).

Sparks pop in and out of view where they cross each other

Symptom: dark holes in the fountain that move every frame.
Cause: the particle pipeline writes depth, so each spark occludes those behind it, and the list's order — which decides who was first — changes every frame.
Fix: depth test on, depth write off (§8.3).

SDL_CreateGPUDevice failed: Video subsystem not initialized in a headless shot

Symptom: the demo runs in a window and fails with --shot.
Cause: surface::headless leaves the video subsystem uninitialised, and SDL_GPU needs it with or without a window.
Fix: .extra_subsystems = SDL_INIT_VIDEO in the app_config for the shot, as the demo does.

The frame graph refuses to compile the frame

Symptom: compile returns false and the log names a pass.
Cause: one of §11.1's rules: a render pass declared a buffer write, a compute pass declared an attachment, or a compute pass's read-write slots have a gap or a repeat.
Fix: the message names the pass and the slot. Slots are the shader's u registers, 0 to n − 1.

18Exercises

1 — Wind, derived implicitly (guided)

Add a wind velocity w to the emitter: linear drag pulls a particle toward the air's velocity rather than toward rest, dv/dt = g − k(v − w). Derive the implicit step, add w to step_uniforms on both sides without breaking the row rule, and keep §G green.

Solution sketch: solving v′ = v + (g − k(v′ − w))h gives v′ = (v + (g + kw)h) / (1 + kh) — the same factor, with gravity replaced by g + kw. So the cheapest change is not a new row at all: fold kw into the gravity row on the CPU, in make_step_uniforms, and the kernel does not change. The one catch is the birth's pre-age step, which uses its own drag factor; it must use the same folded acceleration. A particle then drifts toward w with time constant 1/k — 2.5 s at the demo's k = 0.4, so a 3 s spark ends about 70% of the way to the wind's speed.

2 — A burst (guided)

Make [R] emit 20,000 sparks at once on top of the steady stream. Decide what the burst's births should look like in time, what happens when a burst exceeds the pool, and what the ring does to the sparks already alive.

Solution sketch: a burst is one window of count births in one step, advancing the clock's serials by count; emission_clock::advance already caps a window at the capacity, so a burst of 100,000 into 65,536 slots emits 65,536 and says nothing — log it instead. Give the burst inv_rate = 0 and every birth has age h: born together, which for an explosion is correct, and is exactly the shell §6.4 removed for a steady stream. The ring's price is the real lesson: 20,000 births replace the 20,000 oldest slots, alive or not. With the default fountain those slots hold the sparks born 2.3 to 3.3 seconds earlier; a spark born t seconds ago is alive while its life, uniform on [1.5, 3], exceeds t, which over that window averages to about 17% — so the burst kills some 3,500 of the fountain's 45,000 sparks early, at the top of their arcs. Compute it for a burst of 40,000, and that is the strongest argument for Exercise 5's dead list.

3 — Show the count without a stall (guided)

The HUD in GPU mode says the count never leaves the GPU. Put it on the HUD anyway, without ever waiting for the GPU: read it a few frames late.

Solution sketch: after the compaction each frame, record SDL_DownloadFromGPUBuffer of the 16-byte arguments into one of three download transfer buffers, round-robin, and keep the frame's fence from SDL_SubmitGPUCommandBufferAndAcquireFence. At the start of each frame, for any slot whose fence SDL_QueryGPUFence reports signalled, map the buffer, read word 1, and release the fence. The HUD shows a count two or three frames old, and the frame never blocks — which is the general shape of every GPU-to-CPU readback a real engine does (occlusion queries, picking, auto-exposure). Check it against --cpu mode's exact count: in steady state they agree within the births of the frames in between.

4 — Collide with the depth buffer (engine challenge)

Make sparks bounce off the torus and anything else the camera can see. In the step kernel, project a particle with the camera's clip_from_world, sample the scene's depth there, and if the particle has passed behind the surface by less than a thickness, reflect its velocity about the surface normal.

Solution sketch: the depth must be a sampled texture in a compute pass — bound with SDL_BindGPUComputeSamplers, t0, space0 before the read-only buffers — so its format must support sampling (6.8's supported_shadow_format asks), and it must be last frame's depth: this frame's is drawn after the steps. Import it into the frame graph so it is stored, and declare sample on it in each step pass. The normal comes from the depth's neighbours: reconstruct three world positions and cross their differences. Then face the honest limits: a particle behind the torus from the camera's view cannot collide with its back, one off-screen collides with nothing, and a thickness too large makes sparks bounce off the air in front of silhouettes. Screen-space collision is a trick that looks right where the camera is looking, which is why it is popular and why a signed distance field is what replaces it.

5 — A dead list instead of a ring (engine challenge)

Replace the ring with the design from Gareth Thomas's GDC 2014 talk: a stack of free slot indices. Births pop from it, deaths push to it, and no living particle is ever overwritten. Keep a CPU twin and decide what it can still check.

Solution sketch: a dead buffer of indices and a counter, initialised to all slots. The step becomes two kernels in two passes: one that ages and integrates the living and, for each particle that dies, does InterlockedAdd(dead_count, 1, i) and writes its slot to dead[i]; one that runs a thread per birth, does InterlockedAdd(dead_count, -1, j) on a signed counter and spawns into dead[j - 1] if j > 0 — a birth that finds the stack empty has driven the counter negative, and a later pass must clamp it back to zero, which is a third dependency and a third pass. Which slot a birth lands in now depends on the order of the atomics, so the CPU twin can check the count alive and the multiset of serials alive — both still exact — but no longer slot by slot. Measure the cost against the ring at 65,536 and 1,048,576: two atomics per particle per step against none. Then make it cheaper with a wave-level reduction, after the ⚠ VERIFY in §14.

19Recap & next — and Module 6's project tree

The engine can compute. A compute pipeline is loaded from code and a nine-number reflection, run as a grid of thread groups whose ragged tail every kernel guards, and handed read-write storage buffers when its pass begins. The particle record is three 16-byte rows because two compilers read anything else two ways — measured, not assumed. Randomness is a hash of a particle's name, PCG's, bit-identical on both processors, where the three popular alternatives are correlated, short of bits, or not even one function. An emitter is a clock that keeps its fraction and a ring that needs no atomics, with each birth placed where it belongs in its step; the step is semi-implicit Euler with implicit drag and a floor whose bounces are as accurate as the step allows; and the particles are drawn as billboards with no vertex buffer, added to the HDR light, tested against the scene.

The count of the living is produced by an atomic and consumed by an indirect draw without the CPU ever learning it, and the draw's order changes every frame in the last bits and nowhere else. SDL synchronises passes and nothing within them, and cycle turned out to be a buffer's load op — wrong for the pool in every frame, as §I's traces show. The frame graph learned buffers and compute passes, and derives that flag and the read-write bindings from the same declarations it derived load ops from; the demo is the first program whose frame is declared. And the GPU was held to a CPU specification with honest tolerances: exact where the arithmetic is integer, within 1.67 µm where it is not, and the reference itself no more exact than that across its own builds.
