4Implementation

Six steps. The first four are the new file; the last two are what the rest of the engine had to give up to accommodate it, which is the more interesting half.

4.1  shadow_map learns to be handed a camera

6.8's render fits the box itself, from the same meshes it is about to draw. That was the right call then and it is exactly what a cascade cannot use, since a cascade's box comes from the camera. So split it: the old signature keeps its behaviour and delegates.

Everything else in that function moves into the new overload unchanged. This is worth doing as a delegation rather than a copy: the two must not drift, and the depth pass — Module 2's collect_triangles and Module 3's draw_triangles, still unmodified — is the part 6.8 made a point of not rewriting.

4.2  The split scheme, guarded

The early return for the last cut is not tidiness. Cascade n−1's far distance must be exactly far_d, because the next cascade's near distance is this one's far distance: leave a gap and there is a ring of world that no cascade covers and no shadow appears in; leave an overlap and two maps resolve the same metres. Computing it from the blend would land within a float ULP of far_d, which is not the same as being it.

4.3  The fit

The sphere, then the snap, then the depth range — in that order, because each depends on the one before. The whole function is in §5; here are the three pieces that carry the argument.

Eight points, one average, one max. That is the entire anti-shimmer mechanism from §3.3, and it is worth noticing how little code an idea can be once the idea is right.

z is deliberately not snapped. It is the depth axis, and its grid is the depth buffer's rather than the texel grid's — snapping it would quantise depth for no reason and interact with the bias in a way nobody wants to reason about.

4.4  A depth array, and why the single map became one too

On the GPU, four cascades want four maps. SDL_GPU has a natural home for that — a 2D array texture — and the field that makes one is layer_count_or_depth, a single field carrying two meanings (layers for an array, depth for a 3D texture) because SDL is being honest about a union it did not make one.

Then a decision that is really a decision about how many code paths we are willing to keep in step. HLSL's Texture2D and Texture2DArray are different binding types, so supporting both means two shaders, or one shader with a branch and two bindings. Instead, the shadow map is always an array, even at one layer — which makes 6.8's single map the degenerate case of 6.9's cascade set rather than a second thing to maintain.

A render pass writes one layer. SDL_GPUDepthStencilTargetInfo has a layer field — a Uint8, sitting beside mip_level, both answering "which slice of this texture is the target" — and no way to say "all of them". So four cascades are four passes. That is not a limitation to route around: each cascade has its own camera and its own culling, so they were always going to be separate passes.

⚠ VERIFY, discharged

The plan for this lesson carried a ⚠ VERIFY against the layer field's name, because guessing an SDL3 struct member is exactly the kind of confident fabrication a student would then type in. Checked against SDL3/SDL_gpu.h in the configured build tree: the field is layer, it is a Uint8, and it is declared immediately after mip_level at the end of the struct. SDL_GPU_TEXTURETYPE_2D_ARRAY is the second enumerator of SDL_GPUTextureType. Both confirmed, neither assumed.

4.5  The uniform block, and one field that earns its sixteen bytes

The shader needs, per cascade, a matrix, a split distance, a texel size and a depth range. That is a new per-frame block at fragment slot 2 — separate from the light block for the reason 6.7 gave when it refused to grow the per-draw block: pushes are billed by how often they happen, and keeping the three blocks separate is what lets the biggest one be pushed least often.

Note the shape of the scalar fields. Four floats that are logically an array are declared as one vec4, because HLSL packs float[4] into four separate 16-byte registers — sixteen bytes to carry four, forty-eight of them padding. Lesson 4.6 met that rule from the other side; here it is the difference between 336 bytes and 704.

And then view_forward, which exists entirely because of §3.7's trap:

4.6  The block that must be pushed even when it is not used

This one is a trap worth the paragraph. A cbuffer the shader declares and nobody fills does not read as zero. It reads as whatever was last in that slot — which is a garbage matrix and a cascade_count that could be anything, including a number that sends the shader off the end of its array.

The fallback is a deliberate identity: one cascade, splits past any reachable depth, and 6.8's single matrix copied into every slot. A scene with no shadows at all is still correct, because shadow_strength in the light block is what actually disables the lookup — the same nullable-pointer bargain the rest of the engine makes, with the difference that a GPU uniform slot has no null.

4.7  The shader

shadow_visibility gains a cascade index and reads its two per-cascade numbers from the new block. That is the entire change to the body of 6.8's function — the bias arithmetic below it is untouched, which is §3.5's audit stated as a diff.

Selection and blending go in a wrapper above it, so the sampling function stays a function of "which cascade" and the policy lives in one place:

cascade_pick is a four-way ternary rather than an array index, because indexing a float4 by a runtime value is not something HLSL will do without either a scalar array — which costs the 4× padding above — or a swizzle it cannot resolve at compile time. Four comparisons is the cheaper trade.

7Common pitfalls & debugging

The shadows are correct, but only when you face one particular way

Symptom. Shadows appear and are shaped correctly when the camera looks one direction, and vanish entirely when you turn around.

Cause. The slice corners were built at z = +d instead of −d, so the frustum was constructed behind the camera. −z is forward (conventions §2).

Fix. One sign in slice_corners. The tell is that it is exactly the opposite hemisphere rather than a partial failure.

Snapping is on and the edges still crawl

Symptom. snap = true changes nothing at all — not less crawling, none.

Cause. Almost certainly the basis anchoring described in §3.4: if the light's basis is built at the slice centre, the centre's light-space coordinate is (0,0,0) and there is nothing to round.

Fix. Anchor the basis at the world origin. And test it numerically rather than by eye — take a fixed world point, express it in texels, walk the camera in sub-texel steps and print the fractional part. Snapped it should be ~10−6; if it is 0.499 the snap is not happening.

Objects stop casting when they leave the screen

Symptom. A shadow disappears as its caster slides out of view, often popping back when you turn slightly.

Cause. The depth range was fitted to the slice instead of to the casters (§3.6). An occluder between the light and the slice is outside the slice.

Fix. Extend near and far to the casters' extent along the light axis. This reads like a culling bug and is not one, which is why it is worth recognising by its signature: the shadow vanishes when the caster leaves the frame, not when the receiver does.

The seam is a curve, not a line

Symptom. The cascade boundary is visible and bows outward toward the edges of the screen.

Cause. Selecting on radial distance (length(eye − world)) rather than axial depth (dot(world − eye, forward)). They differ by about 22% at the corner of a 60° frame (§3.7).

Fix. Carry the camera's forward axis into the shader and project onto it. A straight seam is the confirmation.

The shadow map is black on the GPU and fine on the CPU

Symptom. Everything is shadowed, or nothing is, on the GPU path only.

Cause. A Texture2D declared in HLSL against a texture created as SDL_GPU_TEXTURETYPE_2D_ARRAY. The validation layer catches it; without the layer it can silently sample nothing.

Fix. Texture2DArray<float>, and sample with a float3 whose z is the layer index. Turn the validation layer on before assuming it is a maths problem — 4.9's RenderDoc lesson is the other half of this.

Turning shadows off produces garbage instead of no shadows

Symptom. A scene with no shadow map renders with random dark blotches, and what they look like changes depending on what was drawn before.

Cause. The cascade cbuffer was not pushed, so the shader read whatever was last in that slot (§4.6).

Fix. Push it unconditionally, with an identity fallback. A uniform slot has no null.

8Exercises

8.1 — Find the crossover guided

§6 shows cascades losing on a 2.4-metre scene and §C shows them winning 2.8× on a 40-metre one. Somewhere between is a scene size where they break even. Find it: vary the scene extent in §C's setup, plot cascade 0's world_per_texel against the single map's, and report the crossover.

Hint

The single map's texel size grows linearly with scene extent; cascade 0's does not depend on the scene at all (except through the depth range). So one curve is a straight line and the other is flat — the crossover is where the line crosses it, and it should land near the point where the scene stops fitting inside cascade 0.

8.2 — Make λ visible guided

Run the demo at --csm-lambda 0, 0.5 and 1 and record the four split distances and four texel sizes each time. Which λ gives the best cascade 0, and what does it cost the others? Write down the trade in one sentence.

Solution sketch

λ = 1 gives the finest cascade 0 by far and an unusable cascade 3, because the far cascade absorbs everything the near ones did not. λ = 0 gives an almost useless cascade 0. The sentence is roughly: λ trades the near field's density against the far field's, and the right value depends on how much of your gameplay happens within arm's reach.

8.3 — Cache the far cascades engine challenge

Cascade 3 covers thirty metres and changes slowly; re-rendering it every frame is the single biggest waste in this implementation. Re-render cascade i only every 2i frames, and measure the saving. Then find the artefact you introduced.

Hint

Two traps. A cached cascade's matrix must be cached with it, or the shader will sample last frame's texels with this frame's transform. And a moving caster in a stale cascade drags its shadow behind it — which is a reason to invalidate on motion rather than purely on a frame counter.

8.4 — Visualise the cascade split guided

Add a debug mode that tints each cascade a different colour — cascade 0 red, 1 green, 2 blue, 3 white. It is four lines in the fragment shader and it turns every question in this lesson into something you can see, including whether your seam is straight.

8.5 — Break the cancellation open

§3.5 shows the device-space bias is ≈ reach·tanθ/resolution because the sphere radius cancels between world_per_texel and depth_range. Construct a scene where that cancellation fails for every cascade rather than just cascade 0, predict the resulting biases before you measure, then measure.

Hint

The cancellation needs depth_range ≈ 2r. Anything that makes the caster extent along the light axis dominate the sphere breaks it — a very tall scene, or a light close to horizontal so its axis runs the length of the world. Predict with reach·wpt·tanθ/range using the printed ranges.

9Recap & next

What the engine gained.

  • Cascaded shadow maps in both renderers — a practical split scheme, a sphere fit, texel snapping, per-cascade bias and an optional blend band.
  • A depth array texture, and with it the observation that 6.8's single map is the one-layer case rather than a separate path.
  • A stability argument that is measured rather than asserted: 30.2% box variation cured to 9.9 × 10−8; 0.499 texels of grid slide cured to 7.6 × 10−6.
  • An audit of Lesson 6.8 that it passed. Cascades change world_per_texel by 7.3× and not one line of the bias derivation had to move. The surprise was that the device-space bias barely moves either, because the sphere's radius cancels — with cascade 0 the exception that confirms the mechanism.
  • An honest measurement of when not to use this, which is the demo scene this course has been carrying since Module 6 opened.

What it still does not do. Every cascade is the same resolution; nothing is cached between frames; the light is still directional and still singular. Those are 8.3's territory and Module 9's.

Next — 6.10, Mipmaps, LOD, and Anisotropic Filtering. Lesson 3.9 named the shimmering floor and deferred the fix; 4.7 created every texture with num_levels = 1 and wrote "Module 6" in the comment. That debt comes due, and it has to come due before image-based lighting, because a prefiltered environment map is a mip chain indexed by roughness.
