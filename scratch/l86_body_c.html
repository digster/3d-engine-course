5. What GJK actually hands over

Every description of EPA you will read — every paper, every blog post, every implementation's comments — opens the same way: start from the tetrahedron GJK terminated with. It is such a natural sentence that it is easy to read past. GJK's last act on an overlapping pair is to find a tetrahedron enclosing the origin; EPA's first act is to expand a polytope enclosing the origin; the handoff writes itself.

8.5 §9 measured it, and it does not happen.

@@FIG_SEED@@

§D of this lesson's harness takes the measurement again, one lesson later, with the question put more precisely — not “is it a tetrahedron” but “what exactly arrives, by arrangement”: 

  D.1  terminal simplex handed to EPA, by arrangement

       fixture              pairs   1   2    3      4  tetra
       crates on a floor    50000   0  49 49951      0      0
         ...one tilted 1 deg  50000   0   1  197  49802  49802
       free orientations    50000   0  29  755  49216  49216
       ball in a crate      50000   0  63 1242  48695  48695
       capsule vs crate     50000   0  65 1032  48903  48903
       hull vs hull         50000   0   0  204  49796  49796

Read the first row. Fifty thousand pairs of crates standing on the same floor at arbitrary yaw — which is not an adversarial fixture but the default arrangement of almost everything in almost every level — and the four-point column is zero. Not rare. Never.

The cause is 8.5 §9's and it is exact rather than statistical. Two boxes standing on a floor share an up axis whatever their yaw, so their y half extents multiply the same world direction; support_local's >= 0 tie-break picks the same sign for both; and every vertex of the difference set the search visits comes out with w.y = (+h) − (+h) − 0, exactly zero. The search direction is then also in that plane, and the next vertex, and the next. The set is solid and three-dimensional; it is the search that never leaves its equator. Tilt one crate by a single degree and 49,802 tetrahedra appear.

5.1 It is a triangle, not a flat tetrahedron

And notice what does arrive: 49,951 of the 50,000 are a three-point simplex. This is a detail 8.5 could not have predicted and it changes the repair.

The literature's degenerate case — the one the folklore warns about — is a flat tetrahedron: four points that happen to be coplanar. That is not what GJK produces, because reduce_simplex discards any vertex the closest point does not use, and three points in a plane already span everything the fourth could reach. A fourth coplanar point is redundant by construction, so it is dropped before the function returns.

So the input is a triangle containing the origin, and that is much better news than a flat tetrahedron would have been. A triangle GJK reduced to is one whose closest point to the origin uses all three vertices — which is precisely what “the origin's projection lands inside it” means. §E checks it rather than believing it, and it holds 30,000 times out of 30,000. A flat tetrahedron would have needed a choice of which three of its four points to keep, with no guarantee that any triple covered the origin.

The last column of §D.1 is the same data read as a verdict. A textbook EPA — one that assumes a tetrahedron with volume — refuses: 

       CONTROL — a textbook EPA refuses everything but 'tetra'
       crates on a floor   100.00% refused
         ...one tilted 1 deg   0.40% refused
       free orientations     1.57% refused
       ball in a crate       2.61% refused
       capsule vs crate      2.19% refused
       hull vs hull          0.41% refused

Even at free orientations it is 1.57%, which on a scene with a thousand contacts a frame is fifteen contacts a frame with no answer at all. Building the starting polytope is on the critical path, not the error path, and that is why it gets its own section rather than a paragraph.

6. Building the starting polytope

So: a triangle, in a plane, with the origin on it. The polytope must be three-dimensional and must contain the origin. The obvious move is to leave the plane, and the obvious way to leave the plane is one support call along the triangle's normal.

That gives a tetrahedron. It is also useless, and the reason is worth drawing.

@@FIG_APEX@@

The origin is on the base. It was in the triangle's plane to begin with — that is what “GJK reduced to this triangle” means — and adding one apex above leaves it exactly there, on the tetrahedron's bottom face. So the closest face is the base, at distance zero, and the lower bound starts at zero and has nowhere to go: the expansion has no face to push out against, because the face it would choose is already touching the origin.

§E builds both seeds by hand from the public cso_support and measures their starting lower bounds over 30,000 flat simplices: 

  E.2  the starting lower bound each seed gives

       seed              mean      p95       max
       one apex     0.000e+00 0.000e+00 0.000e+00
       bipyramid    9.044e-02 2.234e-01 4.056e-01
       true depth   4.908e-01 9.252e-01 1.000e+00

       CONTROL — one apex puts the origin ON the base, so
       its closest face is at zero: 30000 / 30000 below 1e-6 m.

Thirty thousand out of thirty thousand, and the maximum is zero, not merely the mean. This is not a tail case; it is what one apex always does.

The repair is the symmetric one: take a support point on each side of the plane. Two support calls, four extra shape evaluations, and now the origin has a face above it and a face below it. The mean starting lower bound goes from nothing to 9 cm against a true depth of 49 cm — not the answer, but a real place to expand from.

6.1 And six faces stitched by hand are not a polytope

Here is where this lesson's third bug lived, and it is the kind that is invisible in a code review because the code looks obviously correct.

A triangle with an apex above and an apex below is a bipyramid: five vertices, six triangular faces, three fanning up from the triangle's edges and three fanning down. Writing those six faces out is four lines, and the winding falls out of the order the vertices are in. The first version of seed_polytope did exactly that.

It is wrong, because a bipyramid is convex only when each apex projects inside the triangle, and a support point taken along the plane's normal has no reason whatever to land over the middle of the triangle. When it does not, two of the six faces meet at a reflex edge, the “polytope” is a non-convex surface, and every argument in §4 stops applying — P ⊆ S still holds, but “the minimum over faces is the distance to the boundary” does not.

§E.3 counts it. Convexity is checkable — every vertex must be on the inner side of every face plane — so it is checked: 

  E.3  and the bipyramid must not be stitched by hand

       six faces from a triangle to two apexes is convex only
       when each apex projects INSIDE the triangle:

         NOT convex: 15855 / 30000  (52.85%)

Fifty-three per cent — and only 2.3% of queries produced a visibly wrong answer. That gap is why the bug survived the first full harness run. Half the seeds were not polytopes, and nineteen in twenty of those still came out right, because the very first expansion usually deleted the offending face and repaired the surface by accident. What made it visible was not a failing assertion but a status histogram: 1,172 of 50,000 crates-on-a-floor pairs returning stalled instead of proven, with a lower bound that had gone negative. Counting how your code finishes, not just what it returns, is worth the four bytes.

The repair is to stop hand-building it. There is already a routine in this file that adds a vertex to a convex polytope and produces a convex polytope — it is the expansion step itself, which is beneath-and-beyond and therefore computes the convex hull by construction. So the seed builds a tetrahedron on the first apex, which is always convex because a tetrahedron is, and then adds the second apex through expand: 

    gjk_vertex tet[4] = {pts[tri[0]], pts[tri[1]], pts[tri[2]], up};
    if (!build_tetrahedron(p, tet)) { failure = epa_status::capacity; return false; }

    // The face `down` is beyond is the base — the one whose normal points away
    // from `up`, which after the winding fix is face 0. Handing it to `expand`
    // as the certainly-visible seed is exactly what the main loop does with its
    // closest face.
    if (!expand(p, down, 0, true)) { failure = epa_status::degenerate; return false; }
    return true;

Two lines, and the seed and the loop now share one notion of what adding a vertex means. That is the whole fix, and it is smaller than the code it replaced.

6.2 The other four shapes of input

For completeness, because gjk_result::terminal can carry one to four points and all four occur in §D.1's table:

  • Four points with volume. GJK's good case, and the one the literature describes. Used as is, with the winding fixed by the sign of the triple product.
  • Four points without volume. Does not occur in practice, for §5.1's reason, but the code handles it: of the four triangles those points offer, at least two cover the origin, because either diagonal cuts the quadrilateral into two. The widest one that does is chosen.
  • Three points. The flat case above: the triangle plus two apexes.
  • Two points. The origin lies on a segment of the difference set, which happens on 49 of 50,000 floor pairs. Four probe directions perpendicular to the segment, the widest triangle wins, then the flat case. Four rather than one, because the set can be thin in any particular perpendicular direction and a triangle with no area gives a plane normal made of rounding error.
  • One point. The origin is a vertex of the difference set. Six axis probes, best of six rather than first of six — a first-match loop picks a direction that happens to be barely non-degenerate and hands the next step a segment made of noise.

And if the support function finds no extent along the plane normal at all, the set really is flat — two coplanar triangles, a point against a plane — and its penetration depth really is zero, because the minimum of h over directions includes the plane normal, where h is zero. That is reported as epa_status::degenerate with the plane direction in normal, rather than papered over. A caller pushing along it by zero is doing the right thing.
