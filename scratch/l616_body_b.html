4An honest digression: the far plane is in the wrong place

Check 2 in the list above has a sibling that does not pass as cleanly. The eye should be exactly far behind the far plane — the far plane is far units down the view axis and the eye is at the apex — so signed_distance(far, eye) should be 100.0 for our default camera. It comes out as:

An error of 1.36 × 10⁻³ on a quantity of 100 is a relative error of 1.4 × 10⁻⁵. A float carries about 1.2 × 10⁻⁷ of relative precision, so this is roughly a hundred times worse than the format should give. Meanwhile the near plane is accurate to 3.0 × 10⁻⁷, which is one or two units in the last place — exactly what you would expect.

The obvious diagnosis is catastrophic cancellation in the extraction. The far plane is r₃ − r₂, a difference of two rows, and for our camera those two rows are nearly equal:

Three digits of agreement wiped out. Case closed — except that this course does not close cases on plausibility. So: redo the same subtraction in double, from the same float matrix. If cancellation in the extraction is the problem, the double version will be visibly better.

Seven digits of agreement between the two. The subtraction is not where the bits went. Which leaves only one possibility: the matrix's far plane is genuinely not at −100. The extraction is reporting, faithfully, a matrix that is slightly wrong.

4.1 Where it actually is

A point is on the far plane when z_clip = w_clip. From Lesson 2.10's projection, z_clip = A·z + B and w_clip = −z, so

\[ A z + B = -z \quad\Longrightarrow\quad z = \frac{-B}{A + 1} \]

With near 0.3 and far 100, A = 100/(0.3 − 100) = −1.003009081 in float. And look at what the denominator does:

\[ A + 1 = -1.003009081 + 1 = -3.009 \times 10^{-3} \]

Two nearly-equal numbers, subtracted — in the matrix builder, not in the culler. The magnitude drops from about 1 to about 0.003, which discards roughly eight bits. An error of one unit in the last place of A (1.19 × 10⁻⁷ at that magnitude) is therefore amplified by 1/0.003009 = 332× when it reaches the quotient. Solving numerically:

The full accounting, then: the matrix places its far plane 1.783 × 10⁻³ nearer than asked; the extraction then adds 4.25 × 10⁻⁴ back in the other direction from its own rounding; the net is the 1.358 × 10⁻³ we observed. Two independent errors, one dominant, both now named.

Is this a bug worth fixing? No, and saying so is part of the lesson. A far plane 1.8 millimetres short of 100 metres changes nothing: it culls a sliver of geometry the depth buffer was about to render at z ≈ 0.99999 anyway, and Lesson 4.7 already measured that depth precision out there is worthless — the far 10% of a 0.3-to-100 frustum shares about 0.2% of the depth range. What matters is that we now know which of two candidate causes it was, so that when a real precision problem appears we are not looking at the culler.

It is also a second, independent argument for the reversed-Z depth range the state block has recorded since Lesson 4.7: A + 1 does not cancel when the roles of near and far are swapped.
