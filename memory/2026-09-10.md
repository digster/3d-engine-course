## The lesson's spine

A cascade is a **fit, not a feature**. Each one is an ordinary 6.8 `shadow_map` with a different
camera; nothing about the rasterisation, the depth compare, the PCF kernel or the bias changed.

**The audit is why the lesson exists.** 6.8 derived its bias from `world_per_texel`. Cascades change
that by 7.3×. If the derivation was real, nothing should need to move — and nothing did, because
`light_camera` owns both terms and `visibility()` already read them from there.

**The result nobody arranged:** in *device* depth the bias is constant across cascades 1–3 at
2.031e-03, because `wpt = 2r/res` and `range ≈ 2r`, so **the radius cancels** and
`bias ≈ reach·tanθ/resolution` = 2.0716e-03 predicted, 2% agreement. Cascade 0 is the exception at
64% — its range is set by the casters (31.9 m) not its own sphere (19.9 m), which is precisely where
the derivation says cancellation should fail.

## Two findings worth carrying

**A bug the harness caught that a picture would have hidden.** Snapping did *nothing*, silently,
because the light basis was built at the slice centre — making `basis * point(centre)` exactly
(0,0,0), so there was nothing to round. §E reported an identical 0.499 with snap on and off. Anchor
a quantisation basis somewhere other than the thing being quantised.

**Cascades LOSE on this course's own demo scene**, and the lesson says so. `shapes.glb` is 2.4 m
across with the camera 9.8 m back, so 6.8's scene fit is already tight: cascade 0 comes out at
0.02169 against the single map's 0.0207. There is no far field to over-serve and the sphere charges
its 29% anyway. Cascades pay when the scene is much larger than what you can usefully see.

## Engine notes

- `⚠ VERIFY` discharged against `SDL3/SDL_gpu.h`: the per-pass field is
  `SDL_GPUDepthStencilTargetInfo::layer`, a `Uint8` after `mip_level`. A pass targets one layer, so
  N cascades are N passes.
- The shadow map is **always** a `2D_ARRAY` now, even at one layer, so 6.8's single map is the
  degenerate case and the shader has one code path.
- `cascade_uniforms` is 336 bytes at fragment slot 2, and is **pushed even when shadows are off** —
  a cbuffer nobody fills reads as whatever was last in that slot.
- `view_forward` is not padding: splits are axial depth, `length(eye−world)` is radial, and they
  differ ~22% at the corner of a 60° frame — enough to bend the seam into a curve.

## The decision that had to come first

Unlike every lesson since 6.4, the golden was **not** automatically safe here: the reference fixture
samples textures, so a chain built by default would have moved it. Two honest options — re-baseline
and say so, or make the chain opt-in. **Opt-in won on its own merits**: 33% memory, a 1×1 fallback

type, `texture_binding::mips` nullable.

Making that call *before* writing code was the right order. Written first, the choice would have
been made by whichever was easier to retrofit.

## The best thing in the lesson

**The analytic gradient.** A GPU shades in 2×2 quads *so that* a neighbour exists — which is why
`ddx` is a subtraction and why it cannot be called from divergent flow. A scanline rasterizer has no
neighbour, so the derivative comes from the triangle: `u = U/W` with both affine in screen space, so
the quotient rule gives `du/dx = (dU/dx − u·dW/dx)/W`. `df_i/dx` is `step_x_i · inv_area` — the edge
steps the fill already walks over the area it already reciprocated — and `dU/dx`, `dW/dx` are
constant, so everything but two multiplies hoists. 3.9's Exercise 8.4 asked for exactly this.

## Traps found

- **`max_lod = 0` clamps the entire chain away.** No error — it is a legal request. Same shape as
  `max_anisotropy` being ignored without `enable_anisotropy`. When a feature "does nothing",
  suspect a clamp.
- **SDL's three checks on `SDL_GenerateMipmapsForGPUTexture` all live inside
  `if (device->debug_mode)`.** On a release device the SAMPLER|COLOR_TARGET requirement is
  unchecked and the result undefined. 4.7's exercise claimed that requirement and was right.
- **`rule()` takes a class, not a colour.** Passing a hex string gives `class="#e05c5c"`, which
  styles nothing, so two dashed markers were **invisible for three build cycles**. `check-page.js`
  cannot see a line that was never drawn — only the visual pass caught it. Added a `cline()` helper.
- **Figure numbers had drifted out of page order** (`fig4.png` captioned "Figure 5") because the
  numbers live in `build_610.py` and the order lives in the body fragments. Nothing checks this.

## What kind of lesson this was

**Not a missing feature — a correctness gap in shipped code.** Three facts were already true:
`pipeline_desc` had said "no blending" since 4.4; 6.6's importer read `baseColorFactor[3]` and
ignored `alphaMode`; and that was the *one* gap in that importer firing **no status**. The reason
was structural rather than sloppy — a status says *"the file wants what the engine cannot do"*, and
there was no blend state to be the other operand — so the gap could not be reported, only removed.

## The best thing in the lesson

**Catching the hardware making the same mistake as the naive CPU path.** Lesson 6.1 left a comment
in `scene.frag.hlsl` — *"correct for opaque geometry and wrong the moment anything blends"* — written
when nothing in the engine blended. §H renders the same quad twice, changing only the target format
and `encode_output`, and reads back the centre pixel: 188 and 127. And the shader *cannot* fix it,
because the blend happens after it returns. 42.2% is the same shortfall 6.10 measured in a naive mip
chain, because it is the same arithmetic wearing a different feature.

## Traps found

- **An explicit `destroy()` before a scope ends inverts RAII.** §H segfaulted on exit with every
  check passing: the device was declared first (so destroyed *last*, which is correct), and
  `gpu.destroy()` at the end of the function ran its teardown in the middle of the destructor
  sequence. The shader destructors then released handles on freed memory. Deleting the cleanup
  fixed it.
- **Do not test a symmetric property at its symmetric point.** The "over is not commutative" check
  was written at `a = 0.5`, where both operands are weighted equally and the two orderings coincide.
- **"Empty space" in a plot judged by eye is not a measurement.** `check-page.js` rejected two
  figures with six texts sitting on a polyline. Both now put the legend outside the plot box.
- **A published assertion can legitimately go red.** `verify_67` asserted
  `sizeof(material_uniforms) == 32`; 6.11 spent a third register. Amended to assert what the check
  was ever *about* (the flag is derived), with the supersession recorded.
