## The lesson's spine

A cascade is a **fit, not a feature**. Each one is an ordinary 6.8 `shadow_map` with a different
camera; nothing about the rasterisation, the depth compare, the PCF kernel or the bias changed.

**The audit is why the lesson exists.** 6.8 derived its bias from `world_per_texel`. Cascades change
that by 7.3×. If the derivation was real, nothing should need to move — and nothing did, because
`light_camera` owns both terms and `visibility()` already read them from there.

**The result nobody arranged:** in *device* depth the bias is constant across cascades 1–3 at
2.031e-03, because `wpt = 2r/res` and `range ≈ 2r`, so **the radius cancels** and
`bias ≈ reach·tanθ/resolution` = 2.0716e-03 predicted, 2% agreement. Cascade 0 is the exception at
64% — its range is set by the casters (31.9 m) not its own sphere (19.9 m), which is precisely where
the derivation says cancellation should fail.

## Two findings worth carrying

**A bug the harness caught that a picture would have hidden.** Snapping did *nothing*, silently,
because the light basis was built at the slice centre — making `basis * point(centre)` exactly
(0,0,0), so there was nothing to round. §E reported an identical 0.499 with snap on and off. Anchor
a quantisation basis somewhere other than the thing being quantised.

**Cascades LOSE on this course's own demo scene**, and the lesson says so. `shapes.glb` is 2.4 m
across with the camera 9.8 m back, so 6.8's scene fit is already tight: cascade 0 comes out at
0.02169 against the single map's 0.0207. There is no far field to over-serve and the sphere charges
its 29% anyway. Cascades pay when the scene is much larger than what you can usefully see.

## Engine notes

- `⚠ VERIFY` discharged against `SDL3/SDL_gpu.h`: the per-pass field is
  `SDL_GPUDepthStencilTargetInfo::layer`, a `Uint8` after `mip_level`. A pass targets one layer, so
  N cascades are N passes.
- The shadow map is **always** a `2D_ARRAY` now, even at one layer, so 6.8's single map is the
  degenerate case and the shader has one code path.
- `cascade_uniforms` is 336 bytes at fragment slot 2, and is **pushed even when shadows are off** —
  a cbuffer nobody fills reads as whatever was last in that slot.
- `view_forward` is not padding: splits are axial depth, `length(eye−world)` is radial, and they
  differ ~22% at the corner of a 60° frame — enough to bend the seam into a curve.
