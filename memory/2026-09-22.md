## What the lesson is, in one line

8.10's solver could hold a stack up and could not hold a lamp up; a constraint is written once as
a Jacobian row and two bounds, and rods, ropes, sockets, hinges, limits and motors are choices of
rows and bounds in the same loop.

## The findings worth keeping

**A contact normal IS a row** — `J M⁻¹ Jᵀ` equals 8.9's effective mass to 1.9e-07 on 200 real
contacts. **The row form is 27% faster** (8.75 against 11.95 ns a point per sweep), which refused
the handover's prediction: the row stores `M⁻¹Jᵀ`, and 8.9 recomputes `I⁻¹(r × J)` with two cross
products and two mat-vecs on every visit. Kept hand-written anyway, because the hoist moves every
8.9/8.10 number at the rounding level; it is exercise 3.

**A rope is a contact turned inside out** and lets go at exactly the height a rod's row changes
sign — but both read 17% low at 60 Hz, because the bob has lost 10% of its energy. The parabola
after release lands exactly on the lowest point of the circle (y = L/2 + 3L/2 − 3L = −L).

**A velocity joint drifts and dissipates, both in closed form.** `r(n+1)² = r(n)² + (vh)²` with
`v·r` conserved exactly (tracked to 1.7e-07), and a body on a pin loses **ρ(ωh)²** of its energy
per step, where **ρ = m·d²/(I_cm + m·d²)** — to 0.1% on six configurations.

**Baumgarte cuts both ways on a joint.** On a turning joint it loses *nothing* — its real inward
bias `δ/h`, rotated by `ωh` into the next tangent, is exactly the speed the projection removes —
while split impulse loses `(ωh)²` per step (a 90° pendulum keeps 75.4% of its energy per period
against 92.0%). But a cube pulled into a corner socket keeps 0.35 J of spin under Baumgarte. The
default stays split impulse.

**The block.** Three socket rows contract at their `K`'s Gauss–Seidel spectral radius (0.2323
measured, 0.2325 computed); the 3×3 block is exact (5.9e-07) and cheaper (10.8 against 23.1 ns).

**ρ turns up three times**: a pendulum's period (1.645967 s against 1.646241), a joint's
dissipation, and the per-sweep convergence of a hinge limit against its pin — **0.4261 / 0.7481 /
0.9224** at three offsets, to four figures. A door with no restitution bounces off its stop at
7.5% at eight sweeps. A motor's spin-up is 7.9% slow at the edge for the same reason, and the ODE
with `ρ(ωh)²` in it predicts 2.8847 s against 2.8833.

**What joints leave behind**: a 100:1 chain stretches 388 mm at eight sweeps; eight sub-steps of one
sweep hold it to 20.5 — 19× for the same visits.

## The mistakes that cost the most time

**Instruments reading two instants at once.** The energy check paired an end-of-step velocity with
the end-of-step radius (the velocity was solved at the start-of-step radius): 0.4% off. The period
check used the small-swing limit as the reference for a 2° swing: an error that would not shrink
with `h`. Both are the same mistake, and the tell for the second is general — *an error that will
not converge is a reference that is wrong*.

**Sampling a parameter instead of a phase** (0.42 against 0.50), **a count where a consequence was
the instrument** (the flipped-J control), and **a demo hiding its own point** (motors swallowed the
rebound). All four are in LEARNINGS.md.
