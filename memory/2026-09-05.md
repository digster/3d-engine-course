## The design, in five decisions

1. **One mechanism.** Every binding contributes a signed float; an action's value is the sum. A
   button, an axis from two keys and a scaled mouse delta are the same thing. Holding both halves
   of an axis gives **exactly 0** — a two-kind design would have needed a documented rule.
   Deliberately **not clamped**: two keys on a button sum to 2 (`held()` does not care) and a
   mouse flick *should* be big.
2. **Edges from the action's LEVEL, never a binding's.** Key+mouse on one action: press one while
   holding the other → binding-derived gives a second press and a **double jump**. Invisible with
   one binding, which is what a first implementation and its first test both have.
3. **`consume_pressed()` for the fixed step.** `pressed()` in `on_fixed_step` fires twice on a
   two-step frame *and* is lost on a zero-step one. Queue is **4 deep, overflow dropped** — a
   decision, since an unbounded queue replays jumps after the player stopped asking.
4. **`on_input()`.** None of the seven existing hooks runs exactly once *before* the simulation.
   `on_frame` is the near miss — right frequency, wrong place, 16 ms of input delay.
5. **A concept, not an interface.** `input::update()` samples SDL's live state, so the test could
   not otherwise exist. Beats an ABC three ways: changes nothing in `input.hpp`, no virtual call
   on a per-binding-per-frame loop, and open (`engine::input` satisfies it unknowingly).

**Declined: gamepad support.** Could not be run here; untested device code is a liability that
looks like support. §7 gives the five steps, an ⚠ VERIFY on the SDL3 signatures, and notes that
the hard part (disconnection) belongs in the *snapshot*, not the mapper.

**This lesson measures nothing and says so.** Attaching a number to a decision the number did not
make is worse than no number.
