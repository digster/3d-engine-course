## The findings worth keeping

**The simplex solver was rewritten because of a measurement, and this is the one to remember.** The
first draft shipped Ericson's seven-Voronoi-region `ClosestPtPointTriangle` — the version every
implementation copies. Its three region tests are signed areas, differences of nearly equal
products, and on a thin triangle they go to noise: the region it picks can be wrong and the point it
returns is then the foot of the perpendicular on the triangle's **plane**, which can be arbitrarily
nearer the origin than the triangle is. Measured: **3 failures in 200,000 fat triangles, 1,048 in
200,000 slivers**, worst case 14.5% wrong.

And a sliver is not an edge case in GJK — **it is where the algorithm spends its time**, because the
search's whole job is to drive the simplex onto the closest feature. The failure lives exactly where
the algorithm converges, which is why it survives any suite written against uniform random data,
including this one's first attempt at a "thin" population (not thin enough, and the origin was not
near the plane). Shipped instead: four candidates — the interior projection plus three *clamped*
edges, which already contain their endpoints — evaluated and compared. **21.681 ns per triangle**
against a 115 ns query.

**An invariant you can check is worth more than one you believe, and it pays twice.** `|v|` is
monotone non-increasing by construction (each new simplex contains the last). Two lines test it, and
they caught two unrelated failures: a loop **cycling on ordinary input** (two 3 m boxes 2 mm apart
hit the 64-iteration cap with the answer already correct to six digits), and the sliver bug —
eighteen iterations converging to 0.0233827 m and then one reduction returning **0.0674522**. The
block *restores* the last good state rather than merely breaking, which turns a terminator into a
repair.

**A proof outranks a decision.** A single positive `dot(v, w)` is a complete proof of separation. The
loop banks the best such bound and refuses any later containment that contradicts it — measured on a
sphere and a turned box **55 mm apart** where fourteen clean iterations were overturned by a
tetrahedron whose four face tests all reported the origin inside.

**The shared up axis collapses the search by a dimension, and that is 8.6's problem.** Two boxes on
one floor have equal `y` half extents multiplying the same world direction, and `support_local`'s
`>= 0` tie-break picks the same sign for both, so every vertex of the difference comes out with
`w.y = (+h) − (+h) − 0` **exactly zero**. All 100,000 simplices lay in that plane. **Zero
tetrahedra in 100,000 pairs**, including all 65,633 overlapping by more than 10 cm — against 77,326
once one box is tilted a *single degree*. Every description of EPA opens by assuming the tetrahedron
exists; on the commonest arrangement in a game there is none.

**The seed's sign was worth 22%.** The difference set is centred near `−delta`, so the direction
*toward* the origin is `+delta`. Seeding with `−delta` picks the point farthest from the origin —
legal, and one wasted iteration per query for ever. Mean over 117,413 pairs: **4.52 → 3.52**, and
sphere-sphere 2 → 1. Warm starting, by contrast, is worth **3%** — and the control is what makes
that readable: a *random* seed costs 54% more, so the seed matters and the free heuristic was
already nearly right.

**Polytopes terminate, curves converge — and the odd one out is the sphere.** Box (2.68→2.80) and
hull (1.57→1.98) are flat across five decades of tolerance because a finite vertex set can only be
improved on finitely often. The sphere is flat at **one iteration**, because a ball's nearest point
to an exterior point is on the line to its centre, so the first support call lands on the answer.
Only the capsule genuinely converges (2.06 → 7.43), its difference being a parallelogram rounded by
a ball.

**Two separate things give out at two different gaps, and conflating them would have produced a
wrong fix.** The *proof* first, at `|v| ≈ ε·|w|/tol ≈ 4.8e−3 m` for 2 m cubes — the threshold falls
as the gap squared while its rounding error falls only as the gap. The *answer* second, at
`|v| ≤ tol·|w|` — a contact margin measured by bisection at **2.11 × tolerance × size across four
decades**, which is why `tolerance` is a relative quantity and "set it to a millimetre" is a
category error. A stalled answer is still a valid **upper** bound: worst shortfall below truth over
36,000 pairs, 8.5e−06 m.

**8.4 §11's precision wall is fixed rather than repeated.** Support points taken relative to each
shape's own centre, with the single world-sized subtraction formed once outside the loop where
Sterbenz's lemma makes it exact: **96,270× better** at 1000 km (1.03e−02 m naive against 1.07e−07 m
relative). The two arms are *not* two implementations — `gjk.cpp` is the same code and only the
`convex` view differs.

**And the reference had to be fixed too.** Its first version computed box corners as `centre − half`
**in float**, which is exactly what the naive arm does, so it agreed with the arm it was meant to
convict and reported the relative form as 96,270× *worse*. Promoting to double before subtracting
flipped it. A reference that rounds the way one arm rounds is not a reference.

**A threshold's units decide which constant it may share.** The duplicate-vertex test used
`cfg.tolerance` (1e−4) and was a quiet disaster on curved shapes: a capsule's support point moves
continuously, so consecutive points a fraction of a millimetre apart read as duplicates and the loop
exited early — **2.4% certificate slack where the tolerance promised 0.01%**, on results not flagged
stalled. `k_duplicate_rel2 = 1e-12f`, at float's own resolution.

**The sentinel that was already taken.** GJK's `axis_index` is −3, not −2, because `collide.cpp`
already had `k_no_axis = -2` and `source_of` mapped it to `none` by *falling off the end* of the
function rather than through a named case. Both are named cases now.

## What shipped

Three new files (`phys/convex.hpp`, `phys/gjk.hpp`, `src/phys/gjk.cpp`), a capsule and a `hull` in
`shape.hpp`, `support_local` for every primitive, `collide(convex, convex)` as the façade,
`demos/gjk`, and a 33-check harness in which every separated pair certifies itself from the shape
data alone. `hull` is deliberately **not** a `shape_kind`: it holds a span, and `inertia_of` would
have to answer for it and cannot.
