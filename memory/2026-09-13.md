## Two bugs this session shipped and then caught

- **The boom's composition order.** `L = (H·Rz(β)·S)⁻¹·H = S⁻¹·Rz(−β)` — the inverse of a product
  reverses. Written the way the English reads, the two agree on *x* to four decimals and differ by
  **36%** on *y*. The symptom was not a tilted horizon: `view_from_camera`'s `is_rigid` assertion
  fired, and the **headless `--shot` run hung for ever with no output**, because `SDL_assert` is a
  retry loop and a build server has no dialog to answer it. `sample <pid>` named it in one command.
- **The unit-cube convention, three times in one file.** Cube spans ±0.5 (scale = full size);
  icosahedron has radius 1 (scale = radius). Floor a quarter of its area, boxes half-buried, debug
  OBBs *exactly* 2× too big — which reads as a deliberate collision margin. Answered with `box_at()`.

# 2026-09-13 (second session) — Lesson 7.1: Euler angles, and one numerical bug three times

Module 7 is **open**: 1 of 8 lessons, **76 of 107 published**. `next:` is now **7.2 — Axis-Angle**.

## What the lesson is, in one line

Three numbers become an orientation under a convention the course now *states* — and gimbal lock
stops being a picture of brass rings and becomes `|det(euler_rate_jacobian(e))| = |cos(pitch)|`,
which needed **no new API at all**, because `determinant` has been in `mat3.hpp` since Lesson 2.6.

## The finding worth keeping

**The same numerical bug, three times in one lesson, and it took the third to see it.**
`sqrt(1 - sin²)` for `cos(pitch)`; `sqrt(1 - |sin p|)` for the smallest singular value;
`acos((tr R − 1)/2)` for the angle between two rotations. All three subtract two nearly-equal
numbers near 1.0 and destroy the quantity they exist to report — the first two return **exactly
zero 0.01° before the pose is actually singular**, the third returns **exactly zero for a 0.004°
turn**, which is the step size every path measurement in the lesson uses.

The cure is one shape: get the small quantity from something that *is* small. `hypot(r02, r22)`,
whose length *is* `|cos(pitch)|` — so **the conditioning of the yaw extraction is the length of the
vector whose angle the yaw extraction takes**. `|cos| / sqrt(1 + |sin|)`, from `1−s = c²/(1+s)`.
`atan2(|R − Rᵀ|/2, (tr R − 1)/2)`, whose first argument is built from *differences* of entries.

And **watch the drift before the cliff**: the naive singular value reads 0.0012449 against a true
0.0012340 at pitch 89.9° — 0.9% wrong and entirely plausible. The cliff is what you notice.

## Two measurements that only meant something because of their controls

- **The interpolation check first measured nothing.** Sweeping the yaw alone at pitch 88° reported
  0.00% excess — correctly, because **a one-knob Euler lerp is a geodesic at any pitch**. Rewritten
  to run the *same three deltas* at four pitch bands: **+209.5%** near lock against +26.7% away.
- **A control failed on the first run and was right to.** §C.4 asserted >80° of motion for a joint
  knob move away from lock and measured 65.51°. Two orthogonal 47° turns give 47√2 = 66.47, so 80
  was never reachable. The control told us we did not know what number to expect.
