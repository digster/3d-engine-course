## The two facts worth carrying

**Front-facing is `edge_function < 0`.** The convention is CCW-in-NDC (+y up); the viewport flips y;
a reflection reverses signed area. Measured: a triangle with NDC signed area `+0.18` comes through
the real viewport as `edge_function = −5184`. Do **not** find this sign by trying both — a wrong
guess looks plausible (you see the inside of everything) and leaves two unexplained flips for the
day a mirrored transform arrives.

**The screen test *is* the view test.** `dot(n,a) = det[a,b,c]`, and
`2·area_ndc = kx·ky·(−det)/(w_a w_b w_c)`. Clipping guarantees every `w ≥ near > 0`, so the divisor
is positive and the sign is the determinant's alone. Corollary worth stating: **culling without
clipping is wrong**, not merely unsafe — a negative `w` flips the winding of a triangle that never
moved.

## Three corrections the harness forced

- **"Exactly half the triangles" is folklore.** A cube shows **2..6** of its 12 (mean 5.55), an
  icosahedron **7..10** of 20 (mean 8.80). Never more than half. The eye can lie *between* a
  parallel face pair's planes and see neither — look a cube square in the face and you see one face,
  not three.
- **"Culling is invisible on closed geometry" is also false.** Up to 44 px change over 1,008
  camera/rotation combinations. Rather than tune a geometric threshold I found the threshold-free
  claim: **100% of the changed pixels are ones a back face had been drawn on**. Drawing front faces
  first drops the worst case to 29 px (the silhouette depth tie); the residue is the two outlines
  quantising to integer pixels independently. So culling is an optimisation *and*, marginally, a
  correctness improvement.
- **My identity check was the thing that was wrong**, not the identity. `dot(n,a) == det[a,b,c]`
  "failed" at 1.1e-4 because I normalised by the magnitude of the *result*, and a triple product is
  a cancelling difference — that ratio is unbounded near degeneracy. Normalising by `|a||b||c|`
  gives 1.06e-6.

## Honest numbers

- Forward-axis test wrong on **15.46%** of triangles at 55° fovy, **32.43%** at 120°, **0 of
  200,000** under orthographic — which is *why* the bug survives: it is the ray test, for a camera
  we are not using.
- 54.8% of triangles removed bought **31.6%** of the frame (19.95 → 13.64 µs). Back faces were the
  ones the z-buffer was already rejecting cheapest. Culling is worth more the more expensive the
  fragment work is — which matters in Module 6, not today.

## Caught by eyes, not by check-page
