## What the lesson is, in one line

8.6 returned how deep, which way, and a pair of witness points — everything a solver asks for — and
a crate resting on a floor still will not sit still, because **a contact force acts at a point and
therefore exerts no torque about that point**.

## The findings worth keeping

**A single point does not fail to hold a crate up. It holds one up by CHATTERING, and that is the
uncomfortable half.** Same gravity, same impulses, same normal, only the number of contact points
differing: four points settle at 1.16 mm of penetration and 5.234 °/s of residual rotation; the same
contact reduced to its deepest point sinks **21.27 mm** and rocks at **27.989 °/s**, permanently —
and it reaches roughly the same tipping moment, because it sweeps the face faster than the body can
respond. So the headline numbers are the sink and the rocking rather than the tilt, and the story is
why single-point contact looks nearly fine in a demo and is unusable in a game.

**The statics is derivable before it is measured, and the manifold was never told where anything
was.** The resultant of non-negative normal forces is a weighted average of the contact points with
weights summing to one, i.e. a point of their convex hull, so a body rests iff its centre of mass
projects inside that hull. The moment it can carry is `m·g·(w/2)` — predicted **49.050 N·m**,
bisected over 14 steps at **47.869**, and the 2.41% is the penetration the solver allows rather than
error. Sliding the crate off a ledge, the tipping point lands between 0.45 and 0.55 m, where statics
says, with the support polygon read off the manifold.

**A support function determines a shape and names no feature.** 8.5's claim stands and is not
enough: `support(d)` returns one point when the argmax is a whole face, and 4,000 directions from a
cone 0.001° wide about a face normal return **all four corners** — deterministic *and* arbitrary,
which are not the same thing. The control is what makes it mean something: the same jitter about a
corner returns 1 of 8, so the ambiguity belongs to the **query** and appears exactly where a
manifold is needed.

**And 8.6's own handover prediction was wrong, which was the nicest outcome of the lesson.** It said
to expect `epa_result` to widen and carry the winning feature. `epa.hpp` was not touched: EPA's
triangle is built from *support points*, so it carries the same tie-break ambiguity, and it is a
*piece* of the difference set's face rather than the whole of it. `shape.hpp` and `convex.hpp`
widened instead, by one query each.

**Four is forced, not budgeted.** Four independent non-negative impulses place the resultant anywhere
in a planar convex hull; a fifth is a linear combination as far as the three equations are concerned,
so it makes the answer non-unique rather than better. The same fact as a four-legged table wobbling.
Measured: the reduction reaches **96.316% of the best four there are**, brute-forced over all 70
four-subsets, median **exactly 1.00000**, and loses **zero depth**, because it picks the deepest
point first.

**`face_cos` bounds the normal error at its own arc cosine, exactly.** 44.8024° at 0.5, 2.5611 at
the 0.999 default (acos = 2.5626), 0.2576 at 0.99999. Which makes it a knob rather than a magic
number: choose the largest normal error a solver can live with and take its cosine.

**And the manifold's normal is the reference face's rather than EPA's, deliberately.** On a ball —
EPA's worst case by 8.6 §9 — it is bit-exact (0,1,0) on 4,000 frames of 4,000 while EPA's wobbles
0.001172°. Box-on-box cannot show it: the difference set has a genuinely flat face and EPA
terminates on it, which is why the fixture had to be curved.

**Persistence cannot be keyed on position, and the trap is that a position matcher works perfectly
on a body that is not moving.** Over a crate's landing, a 10 µm match finds **0.00%** and an id
match 82.28%, rising to **100% of 1,999 consecutive frames** once it settles. Hold the crate still,
jitter it 100 nm, and *both* find 100%. It passes every test you would write for it.

## The two mistakes in the instruments, which were worth more than the code

**A control convicted the code of the test's own mistake.** §9's first control teleported the crate
two metres and expected zero id matches; it got **four of four**, and the manifold was right. An id
names a *feature*, and the same corner really is on the same face two metres away. Ids cannot detect
a teleport and should not be asked to. The tell was that the failure was too clean — four of four is
not what a broken matcher looks like.

**And an instrument was a tautology.** §6's first version checked that the two claimed surface points
lie on their shapes and reported a flat zero across four decades of `face_cos`. True by construction:
one point is the incident vertex and the other its shadow on the reference plane. If a measurement
cannot come out wrong, it is not a measurement.

Two smaller ones in the same family. **`acos` has a 0.036° resolution floor** on float unit vectors,
and §6 reported a bit-exact normal as "0.0198° off" for a whole draft — the chord,
`2·asin(|a−b|/2)`, fixes it and reads 0.0000. And **§7's first instrument measured the contact
MIDPOINT against a surface** and reported 0.63 m of "error" that was a fact about the convention: on
a pair overlapping by more than its own size the midpoint is legitimately half a metre from either
surface, and the two recovered surface points come out at 2.52e−06 m.

## A knob was written, measured at zero, and taken back out

`reference_bias` guarded the flip every 2D engine guards against. Measured at **zero flips in
208,000 pairs** across freely rotated boxes, crates on crates and hexagonal prisms — and the reason
is better than the knob: on a face contact both cosines are exactly `1.0f`, so the tie is **exact**
and an exact tie is deterministic. A quantity that merely *ought* to be equal is a coin toss; one
that *is* equal is not. Removed under 8.6 §12's rule. Its control forces a flip by swapping the two
arguments and measures 0 of 4 ids surviving one, which doubles as the warning about argument order:
`pair_key` is order-independent and the manifold is not.
