## The two facts the lesson turns on

**Clip before the divide.** The divide is the one information-destroying step in the pipeline:
`x/w` for a point behind the eye and `x/w` for an ordinary point slightly left of centre are the
*same number*. Nothing downstream can tell them apart, so nothing downstream can clip. It is a
**precondition**, not a guard — `screen_from_clip` has none, and says why.

**`z_clip ≥ 0`, not `w ≥ 0`.** `w = −z_view`, so `w = 0` is the plane through the *eye*. Setting
2.10's depth row `A·z_v + B` to zero cancels `f` and `(n−f)` and leaves `z_v = −near` exactly — the
plane was *arranged* to be a coordinate plane. Measured: a point 0.0001 units in front of the eye
passes `w > 0` and divides to **x = 691,714 px** on a 320-px buffer.

A pleasant corollary worth keeping: `t = da/(da−db)` equals `(z_a + near)/(z_a − z_b)`, because the
`far/(near−far)` factor is common to both distances and cancels. The crossing parameter does not
depend on the far plane, the fov or the aspect at all.

## Verified, not asserted (`scratch/verify_33.cpp`, 9 sections)

- 68,594 random straddling edges: clip-space `t` matches the view-space form to `4.8e-7`; the new
  vertex lands on the plane to `1.6e-6`; its uv matches an independent ray–plane intersection to
  `2.3e-6`; its `w` equals `near` to `1.7e-6`.
- All four SH cases: 3 in → 3 out **bit-identical**, 2 in → 4, 1 in → 3, 0 in → 0.
- **The regression that matters:** 405 camera/tessellation combinations with nothing straddling →
  **0** differing pixels between clip and drop.
- Worked example: `t = 47/60` exactly; crossing `w = 0.3`, depth `0`; and the behind-the-eye vertex
  divides from view `x = +0.40` to `ndc.x = −0.43` — the edge is drawn **backwards**.

## Two corrections the harness forced

- **The winding test was wrong, not the clipper.** Comparing signed screen area before/after used a
  "before" triangle with a vertex behind the eye — whose projection is precisely the garbage this
  lesson prevents. Replaced with a **continuity** test: slide a triangle through the plane and
  assert the sign never changes (502 steps, both windings, 0 flips).
- **"No amount of subdivision removes the artifact" was false.** At 8×8 on the floor, drop and clip
  give bit-identical frames — on a *ground plane* the straddling strip is the one under your feet,
  so once thin enough it falls off the bottom of the view. The measured replacement is stronger:
  over every camera the demo allows, 2 triangles lose the whole frame, 32 lose the whole frame,
  512 still lose **51%**, and it takes **8,192** before the hole is finally hidden.

## `std::clamp` cannot remove a NaN

Every comparison against a NaN is false, so `clamp` finds it neither below the low bound nor above
the high one and hands it back. `float → int` on a NaN is **undefined behaviour**. Three guards,
all written as `!(x < limit)` rather than `x >= limit`: `to_pixel` (±8000 — one constant holding off
*two* UBs, since `edge_function` multiplies coordinate differences in int32), `checker_at` (magenta
on a non-finite uv), and `linear_to_srgb_u8`. The last is engine hardening, not demo scaffolding —
Module 6's HDR pipeline pushes floats through the same function.

## Next

**3.4 — Back-Face Culling.** 3.1 left a measured debt and named the payer: 29 px of painter-vs-
z-buffer disagreement on the solids scene, traced to silhouette ties, with "cull back faces and it
reads 0" already *tested*. The mechanism is built — `edge_function` returns twice the signed area
and `fill_triangle` already reads its sign to orient itself. Two traps to derive rather than quote:
**which sign** (framebuffer +y-down flips it relative to the NDC convention), and **where** (screen
space after the divide, *not* `dot(normal, camera_forward)`, which is wrong at the edge of a wide
FOV). 3.3 preserved the winding so this composes, and verify_33.cpp §F proves it. Culling is an
**optimisation**, not correctness — the other side of 3.3 §3.8's line — and single-sided geometry
(the floor, the quads, the planks) needs `CULLMODE_NONE`, which is why the knob belongs in
`fill_style`.
