## The fact the lesson turns on

**A file's vertex and the hardware's vertex are different things.** OBJ gives every face corner
three *independent* indices; a vertex buffer has one that selects the whole vertex. So a vertex
is the triple `(i_v, i_vt, i_vn)`, and `cube.obj`'s 8 positions become **24 vertices, 16 splits,
0 reused corners** — which is also why a cube in any engine's vertex buffer has 24 and not 8.
Reconciling those two shapes is the first time this course reshapes data to suit the hardware,
which is what an asset pipeline *is*.

## Three corrections the harness forced

- **My hand count of `quirks.obj` was wrong** — 14 vertices, not the 15 I predicted; I had
  mis-mapped the base quad's positions.
- **`torus.obj` has 1,152 `v` lines but 1,150 `vn`.** Not a bug: on a torus `N(u,v)` is *exactly*
  `N(u+π, π−v)`, and in float that survives to the last bit in 2 of the 576 pairs.
- **The seam nearly did not weld.** `sin(1.0f * τ)` = 1.75e-07, not 0, so the two copies of the
  seam would sit an ulp apart and a watertight torus would report 48 boundary edges. Compute the
  angle from `i % nu`, not from `u = 1.0`.

## Caught by eyes, not by check-page — twice, and it said `pass: true` both times

**Figure 4 captioned a fan triangle "leaves the polygon" and a point-in-polygon test put its
centroid inside.** The claim was false. Rebuilding it produced a better statement than the wrong
one had: a fan is correct **iff the anchor corner can see the whole polygon**; convexity is merely
the sufficient condition, sufficient because then *every* corner works. **Figure 5's** two cones
were drawn where the cancellation was invisible; rebuilt so the near cone nests exactly inside the
far one.

And the reverse: the rendered cube's silhouette *looked* concave, and testing every row for
contiguity and both edges for unimodality proved it convex — the "notch" was 3.1's per-triangle
debug palette. The eye was wrong and the measurement was right.

## Next

**3.6 — Normals and Lambert's Cosine Law.** `face_shade()` has coloured surfaces since 3.1 by
indexing five brightness steps with the *triangle number* — it consults no normal and does not
change when the object turns. 3.5 just filled `mesh::normals` from disk and nothing reads them.
Lambert derived from a spreading beam rather than quoted; then the genuinely surprising part, the
**normal matrix**: a normal is not transformed by the model matrix, and the inverse transpose
should be derived from "the normal must stay perpendicular to every tangent". Note the real
constraint that `mat3`/`mat4` have no `inverse()` yet. The scene has two non-uniformly scaled
objects precisely so the bug is visible on a keypress.
