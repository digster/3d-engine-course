## What the lesson is, in one line

Euler's rotation theorem gives every rotation a single axis; Rodrigues performs the turn; and
scaling its angle traces the geodesic — so 7.1's **+14.10%** and **+209.5%** of excess turning
become **0.00% at every pose**, measured on 7.1's own pairs with 7.1's own instrument.

## The finding worth keeping

**One factor settles the design of the entire extraction, and it explains both degenerate ends at
once.** An axis error of φ produces `2 sin(θ/2) · φ` of orientation error — **zero** at θ = 0 and
**two** at θ = π. So the two holes are *not* equally dangerous: the axis becomes unrecoverable at
the identity and it costs nothing, and it becomes hard to find at a half-turn, which is exactly
where the multiplier peaks. A library giving both ends the same epsilon has misunderstood its own
problem.

The strongest form of the argument is that the *placeholder* obeys it: an axis **78.30° wrong** at
θ = 1e-6 misplaces the object by 7.280e-05°, and the law predicts 7.830e-05°. Harmless is
arithmetic, not hope.

## Three times the measurement changed the code, not just the prose

- **A hand-hoisted `sin`/`cos` was written, measured, and reverted** — 11% faster in one harness,
  3% slower in another. CSE hoists pure functions anyway. *An optimisation whose sign flips between
  measurements is not one.*
- **The header's "closed form is 3× faster" was a guess.** Measured 1.3–1.6×: one `sin` + one `cos`
  is half to two thirds of the cheaper spelling's whole runtime and **both pay it once**. Counting
  multiplies predicts nothing around a transcendental.
- **`k_axis_angle_identity_angle`'s justification was an estimate** (1° of axis error at θ ≈ 2.9e-6);
  measured 5.2e-6, scaling as 1/θ exactly.

## A crossover is a band, and a "winner" column cannot say so

The threshold between the two extraction routes is derived from `tan(θ/2) = √3` → **120°**. The
measurement said something better than the derivation: from ~95° to ~140° the two routes are within
25% of each other and **the winner flips from probe to probe**. Outside it is decisive — 6.8× one
way at 30°, **569×** the other at 179.9°. 120° sits in the middle of a band where the choice does
not matter, which is the best place for a threshold, not a compromise.

The first draft printed a winner column and summarised *"the crossover is between 120° and 110°"*,
which is not an interval. **Print the ratio, not the verdict.** And note the harness needs its own
copy of *both* routes — the engine runs one per call, so it cannot make this measurement about
itself.

## The opposite of 7.1's headline finding

7.1 found one cancellation bug three times. 7.2 found a fourth instance of the **identical pattern**
that is benign: `(1 − cos θ)/θ²` is destroyed in `float` (relative error **1.000**) and the matrix
it builds is wrong by **one ULP**, because the term it scales shrinks as θ² exactly as fast as the
error grows. 7.1's rule needs its other half: **ask what happens to the damaged quantity on its way
to the output.** The pattern is the same in both cases; only the measurement separates them.
