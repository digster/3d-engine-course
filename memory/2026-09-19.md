## The findings worth keeping

**The penalty method is not unstable. It is TUNED, and §1's first draft was wrong about it.** Given
164 settings (4 stiffnesses × 41 dampings, 3 s each), the best lands the crate with *zero* residual
bounce — at **108.60 mm** of penetration. The shallowest stable setting sits at 12.55 mm and bounces
**366.75 mm forever**. At 60 Hz you get one or the other. The impulse solver takes both (12.553 mm,
0.000 mm) with no knob at all. What actually kills penalty contacts is **transfer**: the same spring
under a 200 kg crate lets it **962 mm** into a half-metre floor. That is a better argument than the
one the section set out to make.

**The static stiffness is the optimistic number.** `k = m·g/x` gives `ω = √(g/x)` with the *mass
cancelled*, so the allowed sink alone sets the frame rate — 1 mm needs ≥ 49.5 Hz, and 60 Hz can do
no better than 0.681 mm. But a *moving* crate's sink is `v√(m/k)`: 20.0 mm predicted, **44.06 mm**
measured, on a floor tuned for one. Getting 1 mm out of that impact wants `k = 7.85 × 10⁷` and
**990 Hz**.

**The rearranged effective mass proves its own safety.** `dot(d, cross(I⁻¹(r×d), r))` becomes
`dot(w, I⁻¹w)` by the scalar triple product, which is manifestly non-negative for a positive
semi-definite tensor and therefore proves `k ≥ 1/mₐ + 1/m_b > 0`. Over 200,000 random contacts the
two forms agree to **5.016e-07** and the gap **never once went negative** — a check the agreement
could not have made, because both forms would carry the same sign error.

**Momentum is a tautology and energy is the check.** `apply_impulse_pair` uses the same `J` twice
with opposite signs, so momentum reads 1e-7 even when the impulse is wrong. The control settles it:
halve the effective mass on purpose and momentum still reads **1.91e-07** while energy reads
**9.55e-01**.

**A ball that bounces forever, with its height in closed form.** Semi-implicit Euler applies gravity
before the solver looks, so the approach speed is too large by `g·h` = 16.35 cm/s. The error is
*additive* and the bounce is *multiplicative*, so the bounce has a fixed point at `e·g·h/(1−e)`:
predicted 0.1635 / 0.6540 / 3.1065 m/s at e = 0.5 / 0.8 / 0.95, measured **1.000×** at the first
two. That is a 2.2 cm hop at 0.8 and 58 cm at 0.95. And the naive fix — "subtract g·h" — is wrong
for two dynamic bodies, since gravity gave both the same velocity; the control reads
3.066632748 m/s with and without, identically.

**The restitution threshold turns out to be the same fix made approximately**, which nothing I have
read says out loud. Keep both anyway: a 300 N thruster the solver was never told about brings the
buzz back at 9.90 mm and only the threshold catches it.

**Box friction is 42.68% direction-dependent and the cone is 0.00%**, over 361 sliding directions,
with the extreme ratio **0.70085** against a predicted `1/√2 = 0.70711` — and the basis it is
anisotropic in came out of the *floor's normal*. The cone costs 6.8%.

**The slope test disagreed with `atan(μ)` by 3.75° and the fixture was at fault.** A block has two
critical angles and does whichever comes first; a cube tips at 45° and μ = 1 wants to slide at 45°.
A 10:3 slab tracks the formula to **0.43°**. The tell was that the error *grew with the parameter*
(0.0036° at μ = 0.2) — a wrong constant would have been wrong at both ends — and the control was the
same instrument pointed at the other formula: μ = 5 lets go at **45.0032°**.

**The combine-rule default is `minimum`, which Box2D, Bullet and PhysX do not ship.** Five surface
pairs with all three coefficients published: 23.0% mean error against the geometric mean's 119.6%,
and under ±30% perturbation of every number, 20,000 trials, `min` wins **100.00%**. What it costs is
named in the header: `min` is insensitive to the grippier material.

**A sliding sphere rolls at exactly `v₀/(1+c)` and μ cancels** — and the 0.06% the simulation misses
5/7 by is *predictable*, not noise: 8.7 puts the contact point midway between the surfaces, so the
lever arm is `R − depth/2`. The corrected form tracks the measurement to **7.75e-07** across a 150×
sweep the prediction had not seen.

**At one pass the wrong solve order has no friction at all.** Not less: none. 51.2602 mm of slide,
*exactly* what the same slab does at μ = 0, because every frame starts with a zero accumulator and
every cone therefore has radius zero. Two things hide it: iteration, and warm starting (which hands
the cone last frame's radius). And it is invisible on a flat floor.

**And the honest ending: one pass over four contact points does not hold a crate up.** It sinks
**274.57 mm in fifteen seconds** and never stops. Thirty-two passes converge and the depth *freezes*
at 7.2252 mm — the penetration it arrived with. Two different problems; iteration fixes one and
cannot touch the other.

## The two mistakes that cost the most time

**Warm starting inside the iteration loop presented as friction failing.** With sixteen iterations
the inherited impulse was applied sixteen times, and a crate slid **810 mm** down a slope it should
have gripped. The symptom named the wrong subsystem entirely — the spurious normal impulses launched
the crate off the surface, so it looked like the Coulomb clip. Hence `warm_start_contacts` as its
own once-per-step call.

**And a four-degree error in 8.6's EPA that five lessons had not seen.** Two spheres head on: the
manifold normal is **4.2602° off** the line of centres, *constant* across four decades of overlap,
so not convergence noise. It is `epa_config::max_iterations = 32` — 8.6 set it on the evidence of a
worst case of 21 expansions over 200,000 pairs *of boxes*, and a sphere has no flat face to
terminate on. At 64 it drops to 1.06°. 8.7 hides it whenever one shape is a box, because the
manifold takes the reference *face's* normal. Reported and **not fixed**: it is 8.6's knob and
pricing the change needs 8.6's own measurement rerun. It is in the handover.
