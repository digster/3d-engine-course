## The sentence the lesson turns on

**Measure, then act — and write the prediction down first, where you cannot revise it.** Everything
else is the machinery that makes a measurement worth believing: batches not iterations, medians for
frames and minimums for kernels, disjoint zones with the remainder displayed, and a control on
every differential.

## Three findings that contradict the code

- **The 2-triangle floor costs 8× what the 2,304-triangle torus does** — 1,390 µs against 172.
  ns/px are 45.01 and 64.14; ns/triangle differ by 9,300×. The prediction ("the torus, by about a
  thousand") is written into §1 before the measurement, so getting it wrong is on the page.
- **The perspective divide is free** (−0.018 ns/px) and **the sRGB encode costs 6.09 ns/px**, which
  is 2.1× coverage + interpolation + divide + depth combined. 3.2 spent a lesson warning about the
  divide; `to_encoded` has been an unremarked one-liner since 2.4.
- **The clock ticks more slowly than it costs to read** — 41.667 ns per tick (24 MHz counter)
  against 5.42 ns per read. The *cheap* operation is the limiting one, nothing under 4.17 µs can be
  instrumented, and `zone::build` reads 0.00 µs as a live demonstration of that rule.

## Two of my own arguments the harness refused

Both kept **with their results** rather than deleted, because shipping the right decision with the
reasoning that failed is how folklore gets manufactured.

- **The cache argument against the fastest encode candidate is false here.** A 16 KiB table
  measured 0.98–1.04× with four megabytes of framebuffer streaming alongside. The real reason the
  fitted polynomial ships is the *un-rounded* error: 0.0115 codes against 0.4022 — which becomes
  3.0 against 103.4 at a 16-bit target, i.e. the moment Module 6 stops being 8-bit. The
  "worst rounded code" column saturates and cannot see the difference at all.
- **A texture fetch is 2.27× more expensive in situ, not 6.40×.** The first number came from adding
  an encode and two stores alongside the fetch and attributing all three to it. A control that
  removes *only* the fetch fixed it. Second sighting of the pattern 3.9 §5.1 found: when a measured
  ratio is bigger than you can explain, the two sides differ in more than one way.

Also measured and rejected: an exactly-correct encode built by tabulating the 255 **output**
thresholds and binary-searching them. **Slower than `std::pow`** (6.919 vs 3.497 ns) — eight
dependent L1 loads is a longer latency chain than a modern `powf`.

## The result

Fitted four-term approximation (`scratch/fit_srgb.py`, least squares reweighted toward minimax,
constrained exact at white): **1.88× on the function, 1.30× on the fill, 1.29× on the frame**,
Amdahl-checked (ceiling 1.284×, measured 1.287×), costing 2,657 of 473,199 shaded px moving by
exactly one code — 0.56%.

## The debt this adds on purpose

`encode_mode` is a per-fragment branch on a value constant for an entire draw — a compile-time
shader variant in a runtime disguise, and the **sixth** pressure pointing at Module 4's
programmable fragment stage. `fill_style::encode` still defaults to `exact`, because every measured
claim in 3.1–3.9 was made against it.
