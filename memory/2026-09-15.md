# 2026-09-15 — Lesson 7.3: complex numbers, and the half-angle three lessons early

**78 of 107 published. Module 7 is 3 of 8 (~15 h of ~40).** `next:` is now **7.4 — Quaternions,
Derived**.

Planned at 5 h, shipped at 5 — the first lesson of Module 7 to land on its estimate, so no module
or course total moved. Still 107 lessons, ~514 h.

## What the lesson is, in one line

Axis-angle cannot compose; in the plane, composing **is** one multiplication — and the plane turns
out to contain every idea Lesson 7.4 needs, including the `θ/2` and the double cover, in a space
where you can check them with a protractor.

## The finding worth keeping

**The half-angle is a reflection fact, not a quaternion fact.** Reflecting in the line along unit
`m` is `v ↦ m² conj(v)` — rotate the mirror onto the real axis, conjugate, rotate back — so the
mirror's own angle enters *doubled* and nothing was halved on purpose. Compose two and it collects
into `(m₁ conj(m₀))² v`: mirrors `φ` apart generate a rotation of `2φ`, and the object built from
them carries `φ`.

That is `cos(θ/2)`, derivable in two dimensions, measured over 4,000 mirror pairs at **3.8e−06**.
The double cover comes with it for the same reason — `(−R)² = R²` bit for bit, because a rotation
remembers the *angle between* its mirrors and not *which* mirrors.

It also collects a debt 7.2 deliberately left open: that lesson's `2 sin(θ/2)` amplification factor
and its `tan(θ/2) = √3` threshold were both half-angle quantities arrived at for unrelated reasons,
and 7.2 declined to say why.

## The control that carries the most weight

**Reflections do not commute even though ℂ does.** Run the mirrors in the other order and the
rotation reverses — measured `−60.000°` against `+60.000°`. A reflection is a multiplication *and*
a conjugation, and the bar falls on a different factor.

So the non-commutativity Lesson 7.4 has to live with is **already here**, in the plane, in the
operation rotations are made of. That reframes it from a penalty paid for adding dimensions into a
property that was always present and that the one-sided plane form was hiding.

## Three times a measurement corrected a claim rather than confirming one

- **A test on `|z|` certified a broken function.** `renormalised_fast` at `|z| = 2` returns modulus
  *exactly* 1.000000 — the best score in the table — and turns the object **180°**. Not a loose
  tolerance: the wrong observable. The check now asks about the rotation and the page prints both
  columns.
- **The trig-free circle measured 1.21×, not the ~6× the op count predicts**, because
  `z = z * step` is a serial dependency chain measuring *latency* while the trig loop measures
  *throughput*. Four interleaved chains: **3.52×**. The optimisation was real and hiding behind a
  chain.
- **Renormalising fixes the modulus and nothing else.** Over 16.7M steps it holds `|z|−1` at
  5.96e−08 against 3.7e−03 — and makes the *angle* worse, 1.276° against 0.7165°, being one more
  rounding operation per step on a quantity it cannot correct.

## A third category of interpolation, which 7.1 and 7.2 did not have

nlerp **cannot** leave the geodesic — the chord stays in the plane the endpoints span and
normalising changes a modulus, never an argument — so its excess turning is **0.00%**, the same
number 7.2's geodesic gives. What it gets wrong is *when*: `sec²(Ω/2)`, derived from
`φ(t) = arctan(u tan(Ω/2))` and measured at seven arcs (13134.350 against 13131.480 at 179°).

The number to design with is the gap in degrees at the same `t`: **0.13° at 30°, 4.07° at 90°,
26.34° at 150°**. That turns "slerp or nlerp?" into a threshold on the arc rather than a preference,
and explains why a great deal of shipped animation code uses nlerp and is right to.

# 2026-09-15 (session 2) — Lesson 7.4: quaternions, and a type that found a bug

**79 of 107 published. Module 7 is 4 of 8 (~22 h of ~41).** `next:` is now **7.5 — Slerp, and the
Storage Swap**.

Planned at 6 h, shipped at 7 — Module 7 goes ~40 → ~41 h and the course ~514 → ~515 h.

## What the lesson is, in one line

7.3 proved a fourth component is *forced*; this asks what its arithmetic has to **be**, and derives
every entry of the multiplication table from one sentence about space.

## The derivation worth keeping

**Anticommutativity is what isotropy costs.** Demand only that space has no preferred direction —
so *every* unit imaginary squares to −1, not just the three basis ones — and `(i+j)/√2` is one, so
`(i+j)² = 2u² = −2`; expanding gives `−2 + (ij + ji)`; therefore `ij + ji = 0`. Three lines.
Associativity gives the other eleven entries and `ijk = (ij)k = k² = −1` arrives as a *consequence*
rather than as Hamilton's definition.

Most presentations hand you `ji = −ij` as part of the definition, which makes the failure to commute
look like a design choice you have to live with. It is the opposite: a commuting alternative would
be describing a space that is not ours.

## Three facts, one calculation

Composing two reflections in *planes* produces, in one go: the **sandwich** `q v q̄` (because
`n₀n₁ = conj(n₁n₀)` — conjugation reverses a product and negates a pure quaternion, so the two
minus signs cancel), the form **`cos(θ/2) + sin(θ/2) n̂`**, and the **double cover** — as a sign the
derivation *cannot determine*. All three are normally handed over separately.

And `n v n` expands to `v − 2(n·v)n`, which is `vec3::reflect`, written in Lesson 1.8 for a bouncing
ball. Measured agreement 1.046e−06.

## The dividend nobody advertises

**Four candidates that sum to exactly 4.** Shepperd's `4w², 4x², 4y², 4z²` are trace-like
combinations of the diagonal whose off-diagonal terms cancel, so the largest is always ≥ 1 and the
divisor always ≥ 2 — measured smallest pivot over 20,000 rotations, **1.038415**. *No threshold, no
crossover, no bad case.* Lesson 7.2's three candidates summed to 1, guaranteed only 1/3, and needed
a crossover derived at `tan(θ/2) = √3`. **The fourth component is what removes the decision.**

The naive trace route is indistinguishable up to ~120° — exactly where a hand-written test suite
lives — and is off by **180.0°** at a turn of 179.99°.

## Two measurements that refused the claim they were written to confirm

- **§G.3's prediction was that the quaternion route finds the better axis at small turns.** It does
  not: 3.281e−03 against 3.279e−03 at 0.05°. `quat_from_rotation`'s *input is a matrix*; at a small
  turn it pivots on `w` and the vector components come off the same antisymmetric differences the
  matrix route uses. **The information was already gone before either route ran.** A quaternion's
  better conditioning is a property of *holding* one, not of extracting one.
- **§F.4's raw quaternion walk drifts *worse* than the raw matrix walk**, at every step size, where
  the plane's complex number won by 6.15×. An unrepaired norm error compounds as `|q|ⁿ` and the
  sandwich squares it, so the answer is set by one input. One `renormalised_fast` per step ends the
  argument at 6e−08 — five orders better than both — at a third the cost of Gram-Schmidt. **"Cheap
  to repair" is the advantage; "drifts less" is not.**

## The finding that decided the lesson's scope

**The field is called `rotation` and one caller does not put a rotation in it.**
`gfx/renderable.cpp` assigns `linear_of(w.matrix)` — which for an ECS `world_transform` carries the
**scale** from the hierarchy — and writes `1` into `scale`. Its comment says the recomposition
reproduces the affine matrix *to the bit*, and it does, **because a `mat3` will hold anything**.

Measured, and worse than "the scale is dropped": a uniform scale of 2 extracts to `|q| = 1.3337`
whose matrix has determinant **1.6531** and whose pose is **28.15° wrong** — neither answer. A
non-uniform scale *rotates* the result by 1.2089°. Taking the scale off as column lengths first is
exact; a **shear** defeats both routes, because it changes the angles between columns, not their
lengths.

So the swap is ~25 call sites with a design decision inside one of them. **Deferred to 7.5**, with
the reason written into `transform.hpp` where the next reader of that promise will meet it — and
that also resolves the "is 7.5 still a lesson?" question 7.2 and 7.3 both flagged. Not retitled
unilaterally; recorded in STATE.

## The inversion

7.3's sharpest warning does **not** carry up. `renormalised_fast` at `|q| = 2` returns `−q`, and
`−q` *is* `q` as a rotation — so the plane's worst input is bit-for-bit perfect here, and the
damage is between 1 and 2 (34.7° at 1.5). **The failure is not monotonic, so a test at the two
obvious points certifies a broken function.** One level up from 7.3's "choose the observable":
*choose the sample points*, and treat an inherited failure mode as a hypothesis.

The harness also caught a real error in `rotate`'s doc comment: the two-cross form equals
`q v q̄` **only on the unit sphere**, so a non-unit `q` gives neither a rotation nor a scaled one.
A norm error becomes a *pose* error.

# 2026-09-15 (session 3) — Lesson 7.5: slerp, the storage swap, and a broken ruler

Planned at 4 h, shipped at 7 — Module 7 goes ~41 → ~44 h and the course ~515 → ~518 h. **Retitled**

## What the lesson is, in one line

The formula was already derived twice, so the lesson is the two things three lines do not cover:
**the sign the double cover forces**, and **twenty-five call sites** — plus an instrument that had
to be rewritten before either could be measured.

## The derivation worth keeping

**The double cover bounds nlerp's error, and the bound is exact.** `nearest` forces the
four-component dot product non-negative, so the *sphere* arc `Ω ≤ 90°`, so
`sec²(Ω/2) ≤ sec²(45°) = 2` — exactly, over every pair of orientations that exists — and the
positional gap ≤ **8.1491°**. In the plane, where there is no double cover to exploit, Lesson 7.3
measured the same function reaching **13,131×** and **26.34°**. Neither is reachable here.

**The cost and the guarantee are the same fact.** The comparison you have to add because `q` and
`−q` are the same orientation is the comparison that makes the cheap function's worst case
bounded. That reframes nlerp from "an approximation" into "an approximation with a tight, derived
error bound", which is a thing you can build on.

Measured over 20,000 uniform pairs: largest arc 89.9969° (bound 90), largest gap 8.1345° (bound
8.1491). Control without `nearest`: 176.3020°, unbounded.

## The measurement that refused to work until the instrument was fixed

**`angle_between(quat, quat)` scored slerp's own geodesic at −41.10% of its own arc.**

7.4 shipped it as `2·acos|a·b|` — the formula every reference gives. The cosine is *quadratic* at 1
and the sine is *linear*, so a small separation lands inside the `float` ulp below 1 and `acos`
reads the angle off a quantity that has already lost it. Noise floor `2√(2ε) = 0.0560°`; a path
integral over 4,096 steps of a 150° arc takes steps of 0.037°, every one below it, every one
quantised **downward**. Biased, not noisy, so 4,096 of them do not average out.

The repair is the form `axis_angle_from_quat` — **eleven lines above it in the same file** — already
uses *and explains*: `2·atan2(|v|, |w|)`. With it the Euler control lands on **+14.10%**, which is
Lesson 7.1's published +14% arrived at from a different representation with different code.

**The general lesson is not about `acos`.** 7.4 wrote the metric for a *separation*; 7.5 asked it
for a *step*. The function did not change; the question did, and nothing in the name, the signature
or the doc comment marks the difference.

## A NaN never wins a maximum

§G's first table printed a clean `0.0000e+00` for the textbook trig slerp at four small arcs. It was
returning **NaN in 404 of 505 samples**. `std::max(x, NaN)` returns `x`, because every NaN
comparison is false — and the one row that printed correctly made the broken function look *better*
than ours. Count non-finite results; never let one into a maximum.

The cliff was then predicted to three figures: `acos` returns exactly 0 once the dot rounds to 1,
which is within `eps/4`, so `Ω = √(eps/2)` and the rotation arc is twice that — **0.0280° predicted,
0.0279° measured**.

## What the narrower type found

**Three call sites had never put a rotation in a field called `rotation`**, where 7.4 found one and
predicted one.

- `gfx/renderable.cpp` — shipping since 5.11.
- `demos/ecs_swarm` — the same trick, second copy.
- `demos/collector`'s camera boom — **a sheared basis**, shipping since 5.12. It needed
  `S⁻¹ · Rz(−bank)` (scale *first*, because the inverse of a product reverses) and a `transform`
  builds `T·R·S` with the scale innermost. **The repair is not a wider field — it is one more
  node**: put the unscale in one entity and the unroll in its child, and the hierarchy multiplies
  them parent-first. Reproduces the old basis at 0.000e+00.

The three sites collapsed into **one derived function**, `transform_from_affine`, in
`math/transform.hpp`. Writing it exposed an inherited defect: `gltf_view` replaced a zero-length
column with the *parent's* axis and its comment correctly said this "keeps the matrix finite instead
of producing NaNs". It does — and a non-orthonormal basis makes `quat_from_rotation` wrong in
**every** column, 0.399 of entry error on an object whose other two axes were perfectly recoverable.
**Finite is not right.** A cross product of the surviving pair is the right answer, six multiplies.

# 2026-09-15 (session 4) — Lesson 7.6: skinning, and a flag that did not reach

**81 of 107 published. Module 7 is 6 of 8 (~35 h of ~45).** `next:` is now **7.7 — Sampling and
Blending Animations**.

Planned at 5 h, shipped at 6 — Module 7 goes ~44 → ~45 h and the course ~518 → ~519 h.

## What the lesson is, in one line

Skinning has more vocabulary than mathematics, so the vocabulary goes and the one matrix that
matters is derived from Lesson 2.8's naming convention alone.

## The derivation worth keeping

**A skinning matrix is `model_from_model`, and both halves of the lesson fall out of reading that.**
`S_j = model_from_joint(posed) · joint_from_model(bind)`. The inner labels agree, so the product is
legal — and the two `joint`s are the same joint at two different *times*, which is the one piece of
bookkeeping no convention carries and the piece every confused explanation drops.

The *outer* labels agree too, and that is rarely said out loud. Domain and codomain are both model
space, which is (a) why four of them may be **added** — you may add linear maps only when they
share both — and (b) why the sum is not a rotation, because model-to-model maps are closed under
addition and rotations are not. One reading gives the licence and the artifact together.

**And at the bind pose the two factors are inverses, so every skinning matrix is exactly `I`.**
That is the cheapest complete test of a rig that exists: no reference image, no artist, and it
catches a transposed matrix, a chain composed in the wrong order, a stale bake and a wrong parent
index. Measured 4.768e−07 over six joints; control (nudge one bind by 0.01 after baking) 1.056e−02.

## The second derivation

**No matrix is inverted to build the inverse binds.** `(AB)⁻¹ = B⁻¹A⁻¹`, so the inverse chain
composes with the same flat loop as the forward one, multiplying the parent on the *right* instead
of the left. The only inverse anywhere is `local_from_parent` on one node — a transpose, three
reciprocals and a negated offset — **which finally writes the function Lesson 2.8 declined to write
until a caller existed.** Checked against a Gauss-Jordan 4×4 inverse written from scratch in the
harness, sharing no code with the engine: 4.768e−07 at depth 8.

## The artifact, and the module's half-angle for the third time

**`cos(θ/2)`, from one isoceles triangle and nothing else.** Two rotated copies of a vertex are the
same length and θ apart; their midpoint is on the bisector; the bisector of an isoceles triangle
meets the base at a right angle. Nothing in that mentions skinning. At a half-turn the radius is not
small, it is **exactly zero**. Worst gap over a 0–180° sweep: 8.742e-08.

The fix is inside the formula: spread the twist over `n` joints and adjacent pairs differ by `θ/n`,
so the radius goes to `cos(θ/2n)` — 0.000, 0.707, 0.866, 0.924. **That is what a forearm twist bone
is**, and the first one or two do nearly all the work.

**A bend obeys the same cosine on the minor axis of an ellipse** (measured 0.500000 / 0.500000 /
1.000000 at 120°), because a bend rotates about an axis *across* the limb and a rotation fixes its
own axis. One cosine, two geometries — which is why a twisted forearm loses its cross-section and a
bent elbow reads as a crease.

## The one-liner that is wrong in both directions

"LBS is nlerp without the normalise." **Worse**: nlerp's schedule error is governed by the arc
between two quaternions (half the rotation angle, then doubled), LBS's by the whole angle — about
4× at every arc, 10.95° of pose against 2.23° at a 120° twist. **Better**: a rotation matrix is
unique, so there is no double cover, no long way round and no `nearest` to forget. 7.5's 359° bug
cannot be written here (excess over the chord 1.192e-07 over 20,000 pairs).

## The process finding that cost the most

**A flag reaches the translation units it is on and no further.** First Module 7 harness to LINK the
library, and `cmake -S . -B build` leaves `CMAKE_BUILD_TYPE` **empty** — so `libengine.a` carried no
`-O` flag and the first timings were **153.90 ns/vertex against 8.51** and 26.32 µs per palette
against 0.99. ARCHITECTURE.md §6's "-O2, never a debug build" was written about harnesses and had
never reached what they link. The tell was implausibility: 153.9 ns is ~500 cycles for four
multiply-adds.

`build_verify_76.sh` now **refuses** rather than measuring, prints which archive and which build
type, and takes `ENGINE_ALLOW_UNOPTIMISED=1` to do it deliberately. **Use `build-rel/` for any

## What the lesson is, in one line

A clip is a function from time to pose — and because it does not *store* poses, every decision in
the lesson falls out of that one substitution.

## The findings worth keeping

**The per-channel layout LOSES until something is elided.** A naive bake of every channel is
**37,076 bytes against a pose array's 28,520** — 30% *bigger*, because a pose spends 40 B per
joint-frame and three channels spend 16+20+16 = 52, each key carrying its own four-byte time. The
layout is not the saving; it is what *makes* the saving possible, because independent times let a
channel be reduced or deleted without dragging its neighbours. **53 of 69 channels** on a 23-joint
walk carry nothing (one position, on the root; no scale anywhere) and the clip lands at 3,716 B.
Worth keeping as a *shape*: an indirection that costs 30% up front and pays 7.7× once the thing it
enables is actually done. Measuring only the first half would have condemned it.

**A cross-fade is not 7.6's weighted sum.** Blend the poses and every bone keeps its length to
`2.980e−07`, because `quat_nlerp` normalises and a blended rotation is still a rotation. Blend the
composed matrices — identical inputs, identical weight, one step later — and the chain becomes
`Σ cos((k−1)δ/2)`, matched to `4.768e−07`. The angle is the *accumulated* one, so the hips are
untouched and the hands and feet are ruined. Demo: **5.00000 against 4.65020**, and the right-hand
tube is visibly thinner at the tip as well as shorter.

**Root motion is a loop discontinuity by design.** The first seam instrument reported **1.200
units** on a perfect walk. Split `root_travel` from `loop_gap_position` and there are two facts
where there was one confused one. Before adding a tolerance to silence an instrument, check whether
it is measuring two things.

## The measurement that was wrong twice

A 301-key clip came out **faster to sample than a 31-key one**, which contradicts arithmetic. Cause
one: the driver's clock climbed to 666 seconds and **`std::fmod` is not constant time** — its cost
grows with the quotient (1.603 ns bounded, 5.901 ns grown). Fixing it left the inversion in place.
Cause two: it was the first timing in the process and a laptop's first ~100 ms runs 30% slower;
`best_of_three` cannot see past that because all three attempts are inside the ramp. The harness now
spins 150 ms before measuring. **Two independent reasons for one wrong number is the normal case.**

Separately, §4.1's first table varied the wrap period by changing the driver's **step size**, so it
was measuring two things and reported 43% for the wrong cause. Held everything but the length and
the real numbers are 14% and 43% for the search fallback, 2.74× and 10.34× for the cursor itself.

## A defect found in shipped engine code

`quat_nlerp`'s doc comment quoted an error table in **sphere degrees** and then argued from it in
**rotation degrees**. Read as written it claimed a 30 Hz clip's keys are 60° of rotation apart —
1,800°/s, nothing a limb does. Both tables are in the header now, labelled. A quantity with a factor
of two in it needs its unit said every single time, and a comment is not exempt.

## The scale decision 7.5 left open

**Lerp.** Three reasons, hardest last: the arithmetic-minus-geometric mean gap is 0.62% over the
range content uses; `ln(0)` is −∞ and a scale of zero is how an animator hides a thing, so the
geometric rule *teleports* rather than shrinks (and `ln(−1)` is NaN for a mirrored rig); and glTF's
`LINEAR` mode **is** componentwise lerp. The answer to anyone wanting geometric growth is another
keyframe — six of them cut a 59.10% gap to 1.51%. Named `transform_blend`, not `transform_slerp`:
7.5 §12's exercise asked for a name that is a lie, since one field of three slerps.

## The seam 7.6 drew held — and did not pretend to cover more

`build_pose` went from two sliders to two `sample()` calls and a `blend_poses()`, and nothing
between it and the screen changed. What the span could **not** hide is `[M]`, the matrix-blend path,
and it was never going to: that is a different operation on a different type.
